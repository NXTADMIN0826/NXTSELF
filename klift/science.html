Why skin ages

Ageing has many causes. KLIFT addresses each one.

From the mid-twenties, skin loses roughly 1% of its collagen each year. Hyaluronic acid in the dermis also declines with age. Oxidative stress builds up, tone becomes uneven and expression lines settle in.

  • Dehydration & loss of HADual-weight sodium hyaluronate + mannitol
  • Collagen & elastin breakdownGlycine, proline, arginine, hydrolyzed DNA & RNA
  • Oxidative stressN-acetylcysteine, mannitol, niacinamide
  • Dullness & uneven toneNiacinamide, hydrolyzed RNA
  • Expression lines & laxityAcetyl hexapeptide-8, DMAE

01Hydrate

Sodium hyaluronate

Low molecular weight · 100 kDa · 30 mg

Small hyaluronic-acid chains that hydrate and send signals to skin cells, binding CD44 receptors to support fibroblast and keratinocyte activity.

Action in the skin
  • Hyaluronic acid can bind up to around 1,000 times its weight in water, restoring moisture and turgor.
  • Low viscosity lets it spread evenly through the dermis for a uniform, natural glow.
  • Plays a role in regulating the skin's repair responses.
Evidence: Cell studies and the wide clinical use of HA skin boosters.
02Hydrate

Sodium hyaluronate

High molecular weight · 1,400 kDa · 30 mg

Long HA chains, about 14 times longer, that hold water and build a viscoelastic cushion, giving the dermis a supporting scaffold.

Action in the skin
  • Interacts with collagen and proteoglycans to support the extracellular matrix.
  • High-MW HA has been shown to dampen UV- and injury-triggered inflammation via CD44 signalling.
  • Combined with 100 kDa HA, it restores a balanced mix of HA sizes.
Evidence: Cell and mechanistic studies, plus clinical use of HA in fillers and boosters.
03Rebuild

Glycine

Amino acid · Collagen backbone

The simplest amino acid. It sits at every third position of the collagen triple helix, and without it the helix cannot form.

Action in the skin
  • About one in every three amino acids in collagen is glycine.
  • High glycine availability increased collagen production in cell studies.
  • One of the three building blocks of glutathione, the skin's own antioxidant.
Evidence: Established biochemistry plus in vitro collagen-synthesis data.
04Rebuild

Proline

Imino acid · Collagen stability

A main building block of collagen. Converted to hydroxyproline, it acts like a rivet that holds the three collagen chains together.

Action in the skin
  • Proline and hydroxyproline make up about 22% of collagen.
  • Proline availability helps regulate collagen metabolism during repair.
  • Better-built collagen supports a firmer, more resilient dermis.
Evidence: Established biochemistry and a collagen-metabolism review.
05Rebuild

Arginine

Semi-essential amino acid

A precursor of nitric oxide and of proline. It supports micro-circulation and collagen formation.

Action in the skin
  • As a substrate of nitric-oxide synthase, it supports blood flow and cell proliferation.
  • Feeds collagen synthesis through the ornithine → proline pathway.
  • In an animal study, topical L-arginine increased collagen and elastic fibres in older skin.
Evidence: Animal study (mice) plus established biochemistry.
06Rebuild

Hydrolyzed DNA & RNA

Nucleic-acid fragments

Regenerative nucleotides from the same family as PDRN and polynucleotide boosters. They give the skin repair signals and building blocks.

Action in the skin
  • PDRN-type fragments act on adenosine A2A receptors, supporting fibroblast growth and calming inflammation.
  • Supply ready-made nucleotides (the salvage pathway) for DNA repair in stressed, ageing cells.
  • Skin-conditioning, anti-ageing agents that help slow the loss of elastin and limit brown spots.
Evidence: Literature on PDRN and polynucleotides as an ingredient family.
07Protect

Niacinamide

Vitamin B3

The brightening engine. It limits the transfer of pigment to the skin surface, helping tone look clearer and more even.

Action in the skin
  • Inhibits melanosome transfer from melanocytes to keratinocytes.
  • Known calming effects in sensitive, reactive and acne-prone skin.
  • Supports NAD⁺-dependent cellular repair and a healthy skin barrier.
Evidence: In vitro and randomised topical clinical studies.
08Protect

N-acetylcysteine

Thiol antioxidant

Recharges the skin's own antioxidant defence by supplying cysteine, the limiting building block of glutathione.

Action in the skin
  • Helps cells rebuild glutathione and keep their redox balance.
  • Directly neutralises reactive oxygen species from UV, pollution and injection.
  • Helps calm inflammation by inhibiting redox-sensitive NF-κB.
Evidence: Dermatology reviews plus mechanistic data.
09Protect

Mannitol

Polyol · HA protector

Protects the hyaluronic acid. It scavenges free radicals that would otherwise break HA down, and helps limit swelling after injection.

Action in the skin
  • Helps protect injected HA from free-radical degradation.
  • Helps reduce the inflammation and swelling linked to the injection itself.
  • A humectant that works alongside HA's water binding, and has long been used in established HA injectables.
Evidence: Literature review and long use in HA injectables.
10Smooth & firm

Acetyl hexapeptide-8

Biomimetic peptide

A peptide modelled on the protein SNAP-25. It helps relax the micro-contractions behind expression lines.

Action in the skin
  • Modulates the SNARE complex involved in muscle micro-contraction signals.
  • Softens the look of expression lines such as crow's feet and forehead lines.
  • Complements botulinum toxin; it does not replace it.
Evidence: Topical clinical and in vitro studies; independent results vary.
11Smooth & firm

DMAE tartrate

Amino-alcohol · Firming

Puts the "lift" in KLIFT. A choline analogue with a firming, tightening action and antioxidant properties.

Action in the skin
  • Increased skin firmness in randomised, double-blind split-face studies (topical).
  • Helps protect cell membranes against lipid peroxidation.
  • Well suited to the jawline, neck and décolleté.
Evidence: Randomised split-face studies (topical) plus mechanistic data.
