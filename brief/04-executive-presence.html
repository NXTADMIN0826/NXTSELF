How people experience you before they hear your ideas.

TranscriptExecutive presence isn’t a performance. It’s the experience people have when they deal with you. Clarity or uncertainty? Composure or volatility? Here’s a distinction worth holding onto: confidence isn’t certainty. Certainty says the answer is known. Confidence says I can navigate what isn’t. So say what you know, what you don’t, what you recommend, and what would change your mind. That’s precision, and precision builds trust. Presence is also relational: do people bring you hard information, or wait for a safer answer? Before your next important conversation, ask what experience you want people to have when it ends.

Insight

The Perspective

Experienced before explained. Impressions form fast, often before the argument is weighed. Do people experience clarity or uncertainty, composure or volatility?

Confidence is not certainty. Say what you know, what is uncertain, what you recommend, and what would make you reconsider. Precision builds trust.

Composure is not passivity. You can challenge directly and communicate urgency without letting emotion drive the interaction. Conviction plus control.

Presence is relational. Look at what happens to others: do they volunteer hard information, disagree safely, and leave with clarity?

This week

Quick Wins to Practice

  1. Four-line message (5 min). Before a key meeting, write one line each: Context, Insight, Recommendation, Implication.
  2. Say the uncertainty. In your next update, state what is known, what is not, and what would change your mind.
  3. Ask about disagreement. Ask one colleague: “How easy is it to disagree with me?” Listen without defending.
