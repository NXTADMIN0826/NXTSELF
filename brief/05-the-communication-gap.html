The Communication Gap

Why being good at your work is not enough.

TranscriptBeing excellent at your work is not the same as being understood. As responsibility grows, your influence depends on whether others can follow, trust and act on your thinking. Experts tend to add context to prove the reasoning is complete. It’s accurate, and often ineffective. Communication works when the audience knows what matters, why, and what happens next. So lead with the decision, then support it. And listen. Questions, hesitation and repetition tell you whether the message landed. Before your next important meeting, decide the one thing you need the other person to understand or do.

Insight

The Perspective

Information isn’t communication. Complete isn’t the same as clear. It works when the audience knows what matters, why, and what happens next.

Structure creates clarity. Context, insight, recommendation, action. Sequence the information so people grasp the decision before the detail.

Listening is part of the message. Questions, hesitation and repetition show whether your message landed. Adjust as you go.

The gap has a cost. Slower decisions, longer meetings, weaker support for good ideas, and a reputation for being hard to follow.

This week

Quick Wins to Practice

  1. Conclusion first (5 min). Rewrite your next update so the first sentence is the decision or the ask.
  2. Cut to what the decision needs. List your details. Keep only those the decision needs; the rest is evidence you know the subject.
  3. Listen for the signal. In your next conversation, note one question, pause or repeat that shows whether it landed.
Reflect

How often do I begin with context when the audience needs the conclusion first?

Professional influence grows when expertise can travel beyond the person who holds it.
