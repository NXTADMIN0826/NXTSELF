Leadership Under Pressure

The difference between reacting to pressure and responding to it.

TranscriptWhen pressure rises, most leaders do one thing: they step in. More updates, more decisions pulled upward, more oversight. It feels responsive, but it quietly teaches the organization to wait for you. Reacting is immediate and familiar. Responding is deliberate. The question that separates them: will this action improve the situation, or just reduce my discomfort with uncertainty? Pressure is also a diagnostic. It shows whether a leadership principle is a real principle or just a habit on a good day. So this week, when the next urgent moment hits, pause and ask what the organisation actually needs from me.

Insight

The Perspective

Pressure pulls you in. High stakes narrow attention. Leaders ask for more updates and move decisions upward. Managers stop acting without approval, and information gets filtered.

React vs. respond. A reaction is immediate and shaped by habit. A response adds a pause: What is happening? What am I assuming? What does the organisation need from me?

Urgency vs. felt urgency. If every difficult situation becomes an emergency, the organization runs at the speed of your stress response, not the needs of the business.

Pressure reveals defaults. It separates a leadership principle from a leadership habit: collaboration, empowerment and transparency, tested when stakes are high.

This week

Quick Wins to Practice

  1. Name it (1 min). When pressure builds, say to yourself: “Pressure is shaping my behaviour.” Recognising it is step one.
  2. Use the four-step pause. Recognise, Separate facts from assumptions, Assess what the team needs (direction, context, challenge, space), then Respond.
  3. Review one moment (5 min). After your next high-pressure event, write one line: what did my behaviour make more likely in my team?
Reflect

Am I responding to the urgency I feel, or the urgency the situation requires?

Leadership development begins with understanding how pressure shapes the way we lead.
