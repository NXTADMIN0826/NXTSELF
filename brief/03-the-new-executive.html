How AI is changing what leadership actually means.

TranscriptAI is making information cheap. Summaries, patterns, analysis, all faster. That shifts where executives add value, from having the information to having the judgement. A system can give you a plausible answer in seconds. It can’t carry the accountability for the choice. So the leader’s job is to frame the right question, test the assumptions, and own the decision. The risk is treating output as a substitute for judgement. This week, take one AI-supported recommendation and ask: what is this missing, and who owns the call?

Insight

The Perspective

From information to judgement. As information gets easier to get, the quality of your questions matters more. Information describes what is happening. It doesn’t decide what to do.

When the work changes, leadership changes. Roles and workflows shift. Leaders need clarity on ownership, escalation, review and decision rights. This is organisational design, not just tool adoption.

Human capabilities matter more. Judgement, communication, emotional intelligence, adaptability, and building trust without pretending to certainty.

Don’t swap output for judgement. A system can answer quickly without resolving the management question, and it carries no accountability for the choice.

This week

Quick Wins to Practice

  1. Question one AI output (2 min). Ask: What assumptions sit behind this? What could it be missing? Which consequences are hard to quantify?
  2. Name the owner. For one AI-supported decision, write down who is accountable when the system is wrong or uncertain.
  3. Sort one workflow. Mark each step: automate, human-led, or both. Make the human judgement points explicit.
Reflect

Where is information becoming more abundant while judgement stays constrained?
