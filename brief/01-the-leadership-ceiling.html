The Leadership Ceiling

When the behaviours that built your success begin to limit your effectiveness.

TranscriptHere’s a pattern worth naming for senior leaders: the habits that got you promoted quietly stop working at the next level. Ownership turns into holding on. High standards turn into stepping in. Speed turns into listening less. None of these are weaknesses — they’re strengths, stretched past their context. At this level, the question shifts from “How do I make this better?” to “What needs to change so my team can make this better without me?” That’s harder, because results feel further away. But it’s where real leverage lives. This week, look at one strength and ask what it’s costing the people around you.

Insight

The Perspective

Strengths become constraints. Ownership, high standards and decisiveness got you here. Overextended, they become reluctance to delegate, constant intervention and too little consultation. Teams turn cautious, decisions slow, and responsibility drifts upward.

From performance to leverage. Your highest-value work shifts from fixing things to improving the environment others work in: clear priorities, real accountability, capable leaders, early escalation. The question changes from “How can I make this better?” to “What needs to change so the organisation can?”

Intention vs. impact. Close involvement may feel like support but land as mistrust. Fast decisions may feel confident but land as closed. Others’ perception isn’t always right, but the gap shapes what information reaches you.

The ceiling is a signal, not a stop. Growth hasn’t ended. The definition of effective leadership has changed, and that is no failure.

This week

Quick Wins to Practice

  1. Strength audit (5 min). List your top three strengths. Beside each, write one way it may be overused.
  2. Hand one decision down. Pick one decision this week. Define the outcome you need, not the method, and let someone else own it.
  3. Ask for the gap. Ask one trusted colleague: “What is it like to work with me under pressure?” Listen. Don’t defend.
Reflect

Where does my involvement create value, and where does it quietly reduce ownership?

Leadership growth begins with examining how we lead.
